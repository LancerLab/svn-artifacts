  1079	The systems closest to ours keep symbolic shape information but provide no
  1080	audited accounting of which safety obligations are discharged where. Triton
  1081	offers no automatic shape verification; safety assertions are the
  1082	programmer's responsibility. TileLang~\cite{wang2026tilelang} targets
  1083	programmability and performance for neural kernels and leaves checking to the
  1084	underlying pipeline. Halide~\cite{ragan2013halide} infers bounds to serve
  1085	scheduling and buffer sizing, not to audit safety. The \sys surface language
  1086	and its autonomous-tuning pipeline use compile-time \emph{rejection}
  1087	as binary feasibility feedback for a tuning loop, while the present paper
  1088	contributes the per-obligation safety accounting --- generation, discharge,
  1089	refutation, and budgeting --- that the tuning loop implicitly relies on. Code
  1090	generators for tensor cores must still satisfy one narrow hardware contract ---
  1091	the instruction's alignment and shape-divisibility constraints, whose
  1092	violation (e.g., WGMMA shape divisibility) is an empirically documented
  1093	device-specific bug source~\cite{rathnasuriya2026tilebugs}; our hw-constraint
  1094	class generalizes this single contract into a complete, audited obligation
  1095	category.
  1096	
  1097	\paragraph{Graph-level frameworks and IRs.}
  1098	Machine-learning frameworks check shapes at the graph level: TensorFlow
  1099	propagates partial shapes at graph construction, PyTorch traces with fully
  1100	dynamic shapes, and JAX inherits XLA's symbolic shape machinery with
  1101	trace-time checks~\cite{abadi2016tensorflow,paszke2019pytorch,bradbury2018jax,xla}.
  1102	These are per-operator API checks on the graph being built; they say nothing
  1103	about the interior of any kernel. Relay and Relax track symbolic shape
  1104	variables across operators~\cite{roesch2018relay,lai2023relax}, and TVM uses
  1105	them for optimization~\cite{chen2018tvm}, but none maintains an audited
  1106	account of which safety checks are discharged and where. Dynamic-shape compilers such as DISC collect and propagate shape constraints for host--device co-optimization~\cite{zhu2021disc}, again at graph granularity. MLIR carries the most developed IR-level shape machinery:
  1107	its affine dialect supports Presburger reasoning over loop bounds and
  1108	indices~\cite{lattner2021mlir}. It operates after semantic lowering, however,
  1109	by which point cross-tensor contracts (``the contraction dims of a matmul
  1110	must agree'') have dissolved into scalar index arithmetic; IREE adds
  1111	host-side entry assertions but no interior checks~\cite{iree}.
  1112	
  1113	\paragraph{Bounds-check elimination and assertion systems.}
  1114	Static, dynamic, and hybrid elimination of array bounds checks is classical.
  1115	ABCD eliminates programmer-written checks on demand via inequality
  1116	propagation over SSA graphs~\cite{bodik2000abcd}; dependent-type systems
  1117	eliminate them statically through proof-carrying
  1118	annotations~\cite{xi1998eliminating,xi1999dependent}. We differ in both
  1119	directions from this line of work: obligations are \emph{generated} from
  1120	kernel-construct semantics rather than written by programmers, and discharge
  1121	happens while parallel structure is still visible as type-level facts rather
  1122	than over a general-purpose IR. SCuBA demonstrates the cost of working after
  1123	the fact: to find elusive out-of-bounds accesses in CUDA programs it must
  1124	\emph{reconstruct} semantic relations among allocation sizes, grid
  1125	dimensions, and offsets from program code, discharging them with a SAT
  1126	solver~\cite{ghosh2026scuba}. Our RQ6 audit (\S\ref{subsec:e3}) shows why
  1127	this reconstruction is expensive: the relations SCuBA solves for exist as
  1128	declared iterator bounds before lowering, where two classical mechanisms
  1129	settle them without a solver.
  1130	
  1131	\paragraph{Dependent types and shape-typed array languages.}
  1132	Staged shape-dependent
  1133	types~\cite{suwa2026staged} check tensor shapes at compile time through
  1134	staging; size-dependent type systems for GPU array
  1135	languages follow the same discipline~\cite{henriksen2021size,bailly2023shape},
  1136	building on the
  1137	classical dependent-typing line~\cite{xi1998eliminating,xi1999dependent}.
  1138	Refinement types~\cite{vazou2014refinement} generalize further still. The shared cost is a
  1139	programmer-facing type discipline: shape constraints must be expressible in
  1140	the type language, inference is best-effort, and failure is a type error ---
  1141	there is no notion of a budgeted, accounted runtime residue. Our obligations
  1142	require no type-level programming: they are generated from constructs and
  1143	discharged as a byproduct of inference the compiler already performs.
  1144	
  1145	\paragraph{Contracts and gradual checking.}
  1146	Software contracts~\cite{findler2002contracts} and gradual typing with
  1147	blame~\cite{siek2006gradual,wadler2009blame} explore the same static/dynamic
  1148	boundary as our cost-gated materialization, at the granularity of annotated
  1149	program points; gradual tensor shape checking~\cite{hattori2023gradual} and
  1150	gradual shape analysis for ML frameworks~\cite{migeed2024shape} apply this to
  1151	tensor shapes specifically. Gradual verification~\cite{bader2018gradual}
  1152	applies the same static/dynamic split to correctness specifications, letting a
  1153	program carry both statically-verified and dynamically-checked parts. The
  1154	auditor differs in what crosses the boundary and how it is tracked: the
  1155	residue is split \emph{per generated obligation} (not per annotated program
  1156	point), priced by a cost model, and the split is machine-checkable in the
  1157	ledger.
  1158	
  1159	\paragraph{Verification and testing of GPU kernels.}
  1160	The classical line checks CUDA source by concolic test
  1161	generation~\cite{li2012gklee} or by SMT-based deductive verification over
  1162	annotated programs~\cite{betts2012gpuverify};
  1163	ProofWright makes this agentic, inferring verifier annotations for
  1164	LLM-generated kernels at about three minutes per
  1165	kernel~\cite{chatterjee2025proofwright}; and contract-style adversarial
  1166	testing hardens the runtime oracle itself, finding that 39.5\% of
  1167	harness-accepted LLM kernels are
  1168	broken~\cite{shah2026contract}. Controlled studies corroborate that the
  1169	fixed-shape allclose oracle certifies silently-wrong
  1170	kernels~\cite{sarkar2026correctness}. All of this work is
  1171	post-hoc and external to the compiler: obligations are written or inferred
  1172	per kernel, at per-kernel cost, with no total accounting of what was checked.
  1173	The auditor's obligations are generated by the compiler that already knows
  1174	the constructs, and most are discharged for free during compilation.
  1175	Equivalence checkers for tensor-program
  1176	optimizers---superoptimizer verifiers~\cite{wu2025mirage} and graph-rewrite
  1177	verifiers such as TensorRight~\cite{arora2025tensorright}---answer a different
  1178	question, that a transformation preserves semantics, at search time rather
  1179	than compile time, and say nothing about the safety of the kernel itself.
  1180	
  1181	\paragraph{Empirical studies of compiler bugs.}
  1182	Recent studies characterize the failures the auditor targets ---
  1183	tile-program codegen bugs coupled to input shapes, types, and backend
  1184	targets~\cite{rathnasuriya2026tilebugs}, and analogous bugs in FPGA
  1185	high-level-synthesis tools~\cite{wang2025bambu} --- and call for
  1186	in-compiler checking; the auditor is that tooling.
  1187	
  1188	\paragraph{Compiler trust and certified artifacts.}
  1189	Orthogonal to verifying kernels is verifying the compiler itself. The
  1190	certified-compiler line proves correctness of compilation end-to-end, as in
  1191	CompCert~\cite{leroy2009formal}, while proof-carrying
  1192	code~\cite{necula1997pcc} ships a machine-checkable proof that generated code
  1193	satisfies a safety policy. The auditor occupies a cheaper point between these
  1194	two: it neither proves the compiler nor carries full program proofs, but
  1195	produces a ledger accounting for every generated obligation, so a consumer can
  1196	audit \emph{what was checked where} without trusting the discharge machinery.
  1197	
  1198	\paragraph{Runtime GPU safety.}
  1199	Empirical studies of CUDA programs document memory- and shape-related
  1200	defects as a recurring bug class~\cite{wu2019cuda}. Runtime tools such as
  1201	compute-sanitizer~\cite{nvidia2024sanitizer} and hardware-assisted schemes
  1202	such as GPUArmor~\cite{ziad2025gpuarmor} catch violations dynamically, only on
  1203	exercised inputs and at execution cost; Tensfa detects and repairs

  1205	are complements, not competitors: proven obligations need no runtime checks
  1206	at all, and the budgeted residue pinpoints exactly where dynamic checking
  1207	remains necessary.

  1248	\section{Conclusion}
  1249	\label{sec:conclusion}
  1250	
  1251	We have presented the compiler-as-auditor contract for GPU kernels: safety
  1252	obligations generated completely from construct and target semantics,
  1253	discharged by deliberately classical machinery --- constant folding over
  1254	shape-inferred extents and interval reasoning over bounded types --- and
  1255	accounted for in a machine-checkable ledger where every obligation is proven,
  1256	refuted, or cost-budgeted. The evidence over 310 kernels: 93.2\% static discharge, 100\%
  1257	detection of injected shape bugs before device execution, residual checks
  1258	at noise-floor
  1259	cost, and an information-dependence audit showing that every symbolic
  1260	discharge relies on parallel structure that lowering destroys.
  1261	
  1262	Limitations. The evaluation targets one kernel compiler on NVIDIA GPUs, and
  1263	the discharge engine is intentionally incomplete: predicates outside
  1264	normalization and interval arithmetic fall to the runtime residue, whose
  1265	coverage is traded against an explicit cost budget. Runtime overhead is
  1266	measured on sub-second kernels whose residue is dominated by host-side entry
  1267	checks; kernels with device-heavy residues or much shorter runtimes may see
  1268	relatively higher check costs. The audit is limited to
  1269	the obligation classes of \S\ref{sec:ledger} --- concurrency safety and
  1270	numeric correctness are out of scope --- and assessment arithmetic assumes
  1271	mathematical integers. The broader claim for the ecosystem stands on the
  1272	discipline, not the machinery: kernel DSLs already impose exactly the
  1273	structure this discipline needs --- any of them can be an auditor.
